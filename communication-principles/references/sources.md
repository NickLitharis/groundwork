# Sources and Scope

This skill is an adaptation. The source material concerns the output of Unix programs. The skill applies the same principles to the agent's messages to the user, and the adaptation is this skill's own synthesis, not a claim from the book.

## Inspected sources

- Eric S. Raymond, *The Art of Unix Programming* (2003), [full text, archive.org](https://archive.org/stream/ost-computer-science-the_art_of_unix_programming-1/the_art_of_unix_programming%20(1)_djvu.txt):
  - Chapter 1, "Philosophy": Rule of Silence (including the note on the user's attention as a limited resource), Rule of Repair (fail noisily and as soon as possible; a repair that succeeds in appearance but corrupts state is the worst case), and Rule of Least Surprise.
  - Chapter 11, "Interfaces," "Silence Is Golden" and "Applying the Rule of Least Surprise": the attention argument and the cost of novelty.
- Not inspected: the book's style guidance in Chapter 1 and the remaining interface chapters. The prose-style claims in this skill are not from any inspected source.

## Book-derived principles

- Say nothing unless it matters; the user's attention is scarce.
- Fail noisily and early; do not present a partial repair as success.
- Use familiar terms; novelty has a cost.

## Limits and dated claims

- The source material is about program output on terminals with limited space. The reasons were partly historical, and this skill does not claim they transfer unchanged to conversational replies.
- No inspected source addresses language models, agent reporting, or conversational style.

## Modern application

- The "what was verified, what was skipped" rule is an application of the Rule of Repair to reporting, not a quotation.

## Migrated from practitioner style skills (not books)

- "Write plain prose" draws on a plain-English skill that follows the controlled-language style of ASD-STE100 (inspected: its document rules on sentence length, active voice, modal verbs, one word one meaning, and condition before command; the STE standard itself was not inspected). The bans on generated-text habits draw on a rewrite skill that lists AI writing patterns (inspected: its list of patterns and its rule to keep every supported claim). Neither source is a book. The skills' exact word lists and numeric limits are not copied.
